1Wedding vendors on the Main Line
November 2026

Same question, four AI tools, four different stories

Couples now ask AI assistants who to hire for their wedding. I wanted to know which small vendors with great reviews were getting left out, and why. So I built a Python tool that asks Claude the questions couples ask, 5 times each, and records who gets named, in what order and from which websites. Then I asked ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Mode the same questions by hand.

Tools
Python, Claude Code with web search, hand checks in ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Mode
Businesses
5 wedding vendors with 4.5+ stars on Google. Names kept private until they agree to be a case study.
Code
github.com/lakshmimaradana/ai-visibility-checker (business names replaced with placeholders)

The biggest surprise came from comparing platforms. Here's one décor company and the question who should decorate a wedding at a historic mansion venue nearby?

Question: Who should I hire for décor at this mansion venue?

If I had only checked Claude, I would have told this business it had a visibility problem. It doesn't. It's the top pick on three tools, and the real risk is that its credit is split between two names. That changed my message from "let me fix this" to "let me help you keep your spot."

Three reasons good businesses go missing

A single visibility score would hide all three. Each one needs a different fix.

An old name

The décor company: Claude used its old business name in 13 of 15 answers, and the venue's own gallery credits it under three different names.

The wrong town

A florist and event company: its WeddingWire listing has 13 reviews but says Ardmore, with a King of Prussia address. An AI grader refused to count those reviews.

The wrong service

The same florist was named in 5 of 5 Claude florist answers and Perplexity's top florist pick, but in 0 of 10 planner answers, even though it plans full events.

What I did with it

I compared my approach with BrightLocal, Profound and HubSpot's free AI Search Grader. They give a score; none of them showed the florist was missing only for planner questions. Then I wrote to the two businesses, leading with the one finding that mattered most to each, and offered the report for free.

2Chinese Delight, Wayne, PA
November 2026

Named in every answer, but with old prices

I wrote 10 questions real customers would ask about a local restaurant and ran 16 checks across ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google. The restaurant showed up every time. That sounds like good news, but the answers gave prices from 2023 and unsure opening hours, and one told people to skip it or settle.

Delivered
9-item fix list ranked by effort, FAQ copy written for AI answers, Restaurant schema markup (JSON-LD)

I traced each wrong answer back to where it came from: unclaimed menu listings last updated in 2023, and the restaurant's name spelled differently across 6+ directories. One Perplexity answer leaned on Yelp twice, once directly and once through MapQuest.

I compared its reviews with its strongest local competitor (66 vs. 439 on Yelp) and found a selling point no listing mentioned: it's BYOB.
